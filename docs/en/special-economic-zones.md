# Special Economic Zones (SEZs) in Saudi Arabia

> **Educational reference only.** This document describes the structure of Saudi SEZs for informational purposes. It is not legal advice, tax advice, or a guarantee of specific incentives. Verify all figures directly with ECZA before making investment decisions.

---

## Overview

Saudi Arabia operates five Special Economic Zones (SEZs) governed by the **Economic Cities and Special Zones Authority (ECZA)**. SEZs are demarcated areas within Saudi Arabia's customs territory that offer preferential tax and regulatory conditions to attract targeted foreign investment.

The legal basis for SEZ regulations is **Ministerial Resolution No. 468**, published in the Official Gazette on **16 January 2026**.

---

## SEZ vs. MODON Industrial City

| Feature | SEZ (ECZA) | MODON Industrial City |
|---|---|---|
| Governing body | ECZA | MODON (National Industrial Development Center) |
| CIT rate | 5% (industrial zones) | Standard 20% |
| CIT duration | 20 years | Not applicable |
| WHT on profit repatriation | 0% | Standard rates |
| Customs on capital equipment | 0% | Standard rates |
| Intra-zone VAT | 0% | Standard 15% |
| Foreign ownership | 100% | 100% |
| Nitaqat exemption | 5 years (relaxed) | Standard Nitaqat applies |
| Zone purpose | Targeted sector clusters | General industrial use |

---

## Foundational Tax Rule

> **All entities in industrial SEZs pay CIT — regardless of ownership nationality.**

On mainland Saudi Arabia, Saudi and GCC entities pay **Zakat** (not CIT) and foreign-owned entities pay **CIT at 20%**. In Saudi SEZs, this rule is reversed: **every entity pays CIT at 5%**, whether it is Saudi-owned, GCC-owned, or foreign-owned. Zakat does not apply within an SEZ.

This is a critical distinction for mixed-ownership joint ventures and GCC-headquartered companies evaluating SEZ entry.

| Field | Detail |

| Location | Virtual zone, administered from Riyadh; no physical boundary |

| Target sectors | Cloud computing, data centers, AI infrastructure, digital services |

> **Important:** The Cloud Computing SEZ is structurally different from the four industrial zones above. It does **not** offer a discounted CIT rate or tax exemptions. The benefit is a tailored **OECD-aligned regulatory framework** — IP protections, data handling rules, and regulatory clarity for cross-border digital services. Do not compare it with KAEC, Jazan, Ras Al-Khair, or SILZ on tax grounds.

## Common Incentives Summary (Industrial Zones)

The following applies to **KAEC SEZ, Jazan SEZ, and Ras Al-Khair SEZ** (verified). **SILZ** is expected to follow the same framework but is unconfirmed.

| Incentive | Rate / Term |
|---|---|
| Corporate Income Tax (CIT) | 5% |
| CIT preferential period | 20 years |
| Withholding tax on profit repatriation | 0% |
| VAT on intra-zone goods transactions | 0% |
| Customs on capital equipment and production inputs | 0% |
| Maximum foreign ownership | 100% |
| Nitaqat (Saudization) relaxation period | 5 years |

**After the incentive period ends**, standard Saudi rates apply: CIT reverts to 20%, and WHT, VAT, and customs revert to standard mainland rates.

## Important Caveats

1. **Zone-to-mainland sales**: Moving goods from an SEZ to the Saudi mainland triggers standard mainland tax and customs rates. SEZ incentives apply to intra-zone activities and exports outside Saudi Arabia.

2. **Approved activities lists**: Each zone has a specific list of qualifying business activities. Not all sectors are eligible for every zone. Verify your intended activity with ECZA before relying on SEZ incentives.

3. **Land costs not published**: ECZA does not publish a standard land rental rate schedule. Lease terms are negotiated with zone development entities on a case-by-case basis.

4. **SEZ vs. mainland structure**: Establishing in an SEZ requires committing to zone-specific operational constraints. Review zone bylaws and ECZA regulations with qualified legal counsel before proceeding.

5. **Zakat vs. CIT**: Entities in SEZs pay CIT — not Zakat — regardless of ownership structure. This may affect the after-tax returns for Saudi and GCC investors accustomed to Zakat-based assessments.
